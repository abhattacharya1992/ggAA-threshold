\section{Normalisation of the released remainders}
\label{app:normalization}

Five checks establish the normalisation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normalisation} for the tree, one-loop and two-loop amplitudes. They use the tree amplitudes and the one- and two-loop remainders of Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd}, together with the Mathematica code of the original calculation.

\emph{Tree level.} The projections of the two tree diagrams from the original calculation give Eq.~\eqref{eq:born}, after removing a colour factor $\Nc^2-1$ that is included in the file for $T_1$ but not in the file for $T_2$. A direct evaluation of the two diagrams with the vertex $iC_G\varepsilon_{\mu\alpha\rho\beta}k_1^\mu k_2^\rho\delta^{ab}$, which follows from Eq.~\eqref{eq:EL}, gives $|\mathcal A_{\pm\pm}|=C_G^2|\mathcal M_1^{(0)}|$ and $|\mathcal A_{\pm\mp}|=C_G^2|\mathcal M_2^{(0)}|$ to \FeynmanDigits{} digits.

\emph{Tree and one loop.} The coefficient of $\nf$ in $\mathcal M_i^{(1)}$, divided by $\mathcal M_i^{(0)}$, is the same for $i=1,2$ at threshold, and its imaginary part equals $-2\pi/3$ to \CataniImagDigits{} digits at every tested point. This is the value that the subtraction term $2I^{(1)}\mathcal M^{(0)}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:catani} produces through the $\nf$ part of $\beta_0$ multiplying $\ln(-\hat s/\muR^2)$; the one-loop diagrams proportional to $\nf$ have no cut in the $\hat s$ channel. This check fixes the normalisation and phase of the tree amplitude relative to the one-loop remainders.

\emph{Scale dependence at one loop.} Apart from the overall factor $\mA^2$, a remainder with the dimension of $\mA^2$ can depend on $\mA$ and $\muR$ only through their ratio. The released one-loop remainders do not have this property: when $\mA$ and $\muR$ are multiplied by a common factor $\lambda$ at fixed $\bA$ and $c$, $\mathcal M_{i,\mathrm{publ}}^{(1)}/\mA^2$ changes by $-2\beta_0\ln\lambda^2\,\mathcal M_i^{(0)}/\mA^2$, to a relative accuracy of \ScalingRawDev. They are independent of $\muR$, since their eight $\ln\muR^2$ terms cancel. In the original calculation, the amplitude is renormalised with $Z_{GG}^2$, where $Z_{GG}=Z_{a_s}$, and $C_G\propto\as$. With the subtraction operators of Eq.~\eqref{eq:catani}, whose scale dependence enters through $(\muR^2/(-\hat s))^{\epsilon}$, renormalisation group invariance of Eq.~\eqref{eq:perturbative-expansion} then requires
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial\mathcal M^{(1)}}{\partial\ln\muR^2}=2\beta_0\,\mathcal M^{(0)} .
 \label{eq:rg-one}
\end{equation}
The first line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normalisation} adds this dependence, and the resulting remainders are invariant under the rescaling to \ScalingFixedDev.

\emph{Scale dependence at two loops.} The same ingredients determine the scale dependence at two loops. The subtraction operator $I^{(2)}$ of the original code differs from Eq.~(3.4) of Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd} in one respect: it does not multiply the constant
\begin{align}
 H_g^{(2)}&=\tfrac12\Bigl[C_A^2\bigl(-\tfrac{5}{12}-\tfrac{11}{24}\zeta_2-\tfrac12\zeta_3\bigr)+C_A\nf\bigl(\tfrac{29}{27}+\tfrac{\zeta_2}{12}\bigr)
 \nonumber\\
 &\qquad-\tfrac12C_F\nf-\tfrac{5}{27}\nf^2\Bigr]
 \label{eq:H2}
\end{align}
by $(-\hat s/\muR^2)^{\epsilon}$. With this operator, one finds
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial\Mfull{}}{\partial\ln\muR^2}=3\beta_0\,\mathcal M^{(1)}+\bigl(2\beta_1-8H_g^{(2)}-12C_F\nf\bigr)\mathcal M^{(0)},
 \label{eq:rg-two}
\end{equation}
where $\beta_1=\tfrac{34}{3}C_A^2-2C_F\nf-\tfrac{10}{3}C_A\nf$. The last term comes from the counterterm of the $O_G$--$O_J$ amplitude, Eq.~\eqref{eq:gj-proportional}, in the renormalisation of Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd}. I fitted $\partial\Mfull{}/\partial\ln\muR^2=a\,\beta_0\mathcal M^{(1)}+b\,\mathcal M^{(0)}$ to the series at two kinematic points, for $(\Nc,\nf,\mA)=$ \RGSets. With both lines of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normalisation}, the fit gives $a=\RGCoefficient$ to \RGaDev{}, and $b$ agrees with Eq.~\eqref{eq:rg-two} to \RGbDev, the accuracy of the series at these velocities. The second derivative equals $6\beta_0^2\mathcal M^{(0)}$, the value implied by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:rg-one} and \eqref{eq:rg-two}, to \RGSecondDev. Without the division by \FileFactor{}, the fit gives $a=\RGCoefficientPublished$. Without the scale term of the one-loop remainders, $b$ differs from Eq.~\eqref{eq:rg-two} by $6\beta_0^2\ln(\mA^2/\mathrm{GeV}^2)$. The agreement therefore also fixes the reference scale of the released one-loop remainders at $\muR=1$~GeV.

\emph{Origin of the factor \FileFactor.} The code of the original calculation documents this factor~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd}. The two-loop renormalisation step multiplies the $O_G$--$O_G$ amplitude by $\FileFactor=\FileFactorRoot^2$, including the tree and one-loop amplitudes that enter the infrared subtraction, whereas the one-loop step does not. The pole cancellation of Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd} is unaffected, since all terms of the two-loop subtraction carry the same factor. The code and data released with this article use the normalisation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normalisation}.


\emph{The original output.} The Mathematica output of the original
calculation confirms both conversions. Its one-loop remainders, normalised
to the tree amplitude, depend on $\muR$ as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rg-one} and
agree with the first line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normalisation} to
\ArchiveOneLoopDev; the released one-loop files coincide with them at
$\muR=1$~GeV. For two loops, an earlier run of the renormalisation step,
without the factor \FileFactor{} and with the $O_J$ contribution switched
off, was evaluated at $x=1/11$, $z=3/11$. There the real part of the
released $P_1$ remainder agrees to \ArchiveTwoLoopDev{} with \FileFactor{}
times this result, multiplied by the tree amplitude, plus the
$O_G$--$O_J$ counterterm; the imaginary parts differ by $32C_F$ times the
one-loop $O_G$--$O_J$ amplitude, a multiple of the tree amplitude. The
earlier run is not an alternative result: its renormalisation constant
exceeds $Z_{GG}^2=Z_{a_s}^2$ by a $2\beta_1\as^2/\epsilon$ pole and it has
no $O_G$--$O_J$ counterterm, so its single poles do not cancel with the IR
subtraction of the released version. In the released version, the
normalisation of this counterterm is fixed by the cancellation of the
$C_F\nf$ single pole, and a June 2019 record of the original calculation
requires the same factor in both projections at \RenormPointsWord{}
kinematic points. The files and a script that reproduce these comparisons
are released with this article.
