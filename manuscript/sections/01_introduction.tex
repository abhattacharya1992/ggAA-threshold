\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Precision tests of the Standard Model (SM) and searches for new particles
at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) require theoretical predictions
for production rates and differential cross sections. The accuracy
of these predictions is important when comparing different models
or determining whether a discrepancy with a measurement indicates
a new interaction.
QCD radiative corrections in gluon fusion processes can be sizeable, and
calculations beyond leading order (LO) are needed to assess their effect on
the predicted rates and distributions~\cite{Baglio:2023euv}.
Loop amplitudes enter the virtual
contributions to these corrections.
Their calculation requires the evaluation of loop integrals and control over their behaviour in the physical region.
In particular, amplitudes
can develop singularities at special kinematic configurations even
when the corresponding tree amplitudes are regular. The location
and strength of these singularities matter for numerical
evaluation and for the behaviour of differential cross
sections~\cite{GauntStirling2011,DixonEsterlis2016}.
An expansion about such a configuration allows the singular terms
to be studied explicitly, together with the regular terms needed
for a quantitative prediction.

A relevant example is neutral Higgs boson pair production in
two-Higgs-doublet models (2HDM). At the LHC, the dominant production
mechanism is gluon fusion through top quark loops, so that loop
amplitudes enter already at LO \cite{Plehn:1996wb,Baglio:2023euv}.
For pseudoscalar pairs,
$gg\to AA$, the next-to-LO (NLO) QCD corrections were
first computed in the limit of a heavy top
quark~\cite{DawsonDittmaierSpira1998}, later matched to parton
showers~\cite{Hespel:2014sla}, and subsequently obtained with the
full dependence on the top quark mass~\cite{Baglio:2023euv}.
In the benchmark considered in Ref.~\cite{Baglio:2023euv}, the
full NLO cross section for $AA$ lies about $5\%$ below the
Born-improved heavy top quark approximation at LHC energies,
compared with about $12\%$ for $hH$. For this benchmark, the
comparison supports using the heavy top quark limit (HTL) as a
starting point for higher orders, with the exact mass dependence
retained at LO. Beyond NLO, only the two-loop virtual
amplitudes are known in this limit~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd}.
For SM Higgs pairs, the corrections in the same limit are known to
N$^3$LO~\cite{deFlorianMazzitelli2013NNLO,Banerjee:2018lfq,ChenEtAl2020}.
The same effective gluon interactions also contribute to the
production of axion-like particle pairs, and non-resonant
$gg\to aa$ production has been used to constrain their
interactions at the LHC~\cite{Brivio:2026alp}.

%In the HTL, the pseudoscalar couples to gluons through $G\tilde G$ and to light quarks through the divergence of the axial current.
In the limit of a heavy top quark, a pseudoscalar field
$\Phi^A(x)$ couples to gluons and light quarks through the
effective Lagrangian~\cite{Chetyrkin:1998mw}
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L^A_{\mathrm{eff}}=\Phi^A(x)\left[
 -\frac{1}{8}C_G\,O_G(x)-\frac{1}{2}C_J\,O_J(x)\right],
 \label{eq:EL}
\end{equation}
with the gluonic and light quark operators
\begin{align}
O_G(x)&=G^{a\mu\nu}\tilde{G}_{\mu\nu}^a=\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
G^{a\mu\nu}G^{a\rho\sigma},
\\
G^{a\mu\nu}&=\partial^{\mu}G^{a\nu}-\partial^{\nu}G^{a\mu}+g_s f^{abc}
G_{b}^{\mu}G_{c}^{\nu},
\end{align}
where $f^{abc}$ are the structure constants of SU(3) and
$\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$ is the Levi-Civita tensor. The
light quark operator is the divergence of the flavour singlet
axial vector current,
\begin{equation}
O_{J}(x)=\partial_{\mu}\left(\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\psi\right) \,.
\label{eq:Fields EL}
\end{equation}
%The effective Lagrangian is obtained after integrating out the top quark fields in the large top mass limit. Hence, the corresponding Wilson coefficients C$_{G}$ and C$_{J}$ depend on the mass of the top quark $m_t$.
By the Adler--Bardeen theorem~\cite{Adler:1969gk}, $C_G$ receives
no QCD corrections beyond one loop, whereas $C_J$ starts at second
order in the strong coupling $\as\equiv g_s^2/16\pi^2=\alpha_s/4\pi$.
The Wilson coefficients are
\begin{align}
C_{G}\left(a_{s}\right) & =-a_{s}2^{\tfrac{5}{4}}G_{F}^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\cot\beta \,,
\label{WCG}
\\
C_{J}\left(a_{s}\right) & =-\left[a_{s}C_{F}\left(\dfrac{3}{2}-3\ln\dfrac{\mu_{R}^{2}}{m_{t}^{2}}\right)
+a_{s}^{2}C_{J}^{\left(2\right)}+...\right]C_{G}\,,
\label{WCJ}
\end{align}
where $G_F$ is the Fermi constant, $m_t$ the top quark mass and
$\tan\beta$ the ratio of the vacuum expectation values of the two
Higgs doublets, in a model without spontaneous CP violation; this
angle is unrelated to the velocity
\begin{equation}
 \bA=\sqrt{1-4\mA^2/\hat s}
 \label{eq:beta_def}
\end{equation}
of the pseudoscalars in the partonic centre of mass frame, at fixed scattering angle.
$C_F$ is the quadratic Casimir of the fundamental representation and
$\muR$ the scale at which $\as$ is renormalised. The conventions are those of Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd}. Operator mixing, ultraviolet renormalisation and the treatment of $\gamma_5$~\cite{Larin1993} follow the original calculation in Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd}; the same effective theory underlies inclusive pseudoscalar production at NNLO~\cite{HarlanderKilgore2002}.

I consider the dominant gluon fusion subprocess
\begin{equation}
 g(p_1)+g(p_2)\to A(p_3)+A(p_4),
 \label{eq:amp}
\end{equation}
with incoming gluon momenta $p_{1,2}^2=0$ and outgoing pseudoscalar momenta $p_{3,4}^2=\mA^2$.
%
The amplitude for $gg\to AA$ separates into two classes~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd}. In Type~I, both pseudoscalars originate from a single effective vertex. In Type~II, each pseudoscalar is emitted from its own vertex and the two vertices are connected by gluon exchange in the $t$ or $u$ channel (Fig.~\ref{fig:diagrams}a). In the full theory, the Type~II diagrams correspond to two top quark triangles joined by a gluon, the one-particle reducible contributions of Refs.~\cite{DawsonDittmaierSpira1998,Baglio:2023euv}; for Higgs pairs the corresponding class was computed at two loops \cite{Banerjee:2018lfq}. The two-loop Type~II finite remainders for $gg\to AA$ were obtained in closed form in Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd}. They contain polylogarithms of weight up to four, among them $\Li_{2,2}$, and inverse powers of the Gram determinant $tu-\mA^4$, which vanishes at the pair threshold. The expressions occupy about \PublishedTwoLoopMB~MB, and large cancellations between their terms make them hard to evaluate close to threshold.

There are two reasons to study this challenging threshold region. The first is phenomenological: in the NLO predictions of Ref.~\cite{Baglio:2023euv}, for $\mA=134.7$~GeV the invariant mass distribution of the pair is larger at $m_{AA}=300$~GeV, about 30~GeV above threshold, than at 400~GeV, and threshold resummation of this distribution requires at each $m_{AA}$ a hard function obtained from the infrared (IR) finite virtual amplitude~\cite{Sterman:1986aj,Catani:1989ne,deFlorian:2012za,deFlorian:2015moa,Bhattacharya:2021nsv}. The second concerns the analytic structure of loop amplitudes. When the transverse momentum $p_T$ of the final state particles vanishes, a one-loop amplitude with massless internal lines can have a Landau singularity~\cite{Landau:1959fi} in which each incoming parton splits into two collinear partons that then take part in two separate $2\to1$ scatterings.
Gaunt and Stirling showed that this double parton scattering (DPS)
singularity is at most logarithmic in $p_T$ for SM amplitudes and
that, for the processes they considered, it occurs only when the
incoming state has $J_z=0$. They also derived a factorised formula
for its coefficient~\cite{GauntStirling2011}.
Dixon and Esterlis studied the corresponding singularity of six-gluon amplitudes in planar $\mathcal N=4$ super Yang--Mills theory to high loop order~\cite{DixonEsterlis2016}.
However, it has not been examined beyond one loop in QCD at the level of amplitudes for a $2\to2$ process with a colour singlet final state; NLO corrections to the underlying splitting mechanism are known~\cite{Diehl:2019rdh} for double parton distributions~\cite{DiehlOstermeierSchafer2012}.
The Type~II amplitude for $gg\to AA$ contains diagrams with this
topology: each incoming gluon can split into two gluons, which
fuse pairwise into the two pseudoscalars at the effective vertices
(Fig.~\ref{fig:diagrams}b). At threshold, $p_T\to0$ at every
scattering angle, so a threshold expansion probes this
configuration directly.

In hadronic collisions, a configuration in which a parton from
each proton splits into two can enter both the single and
double parton scattering calculations. The overlap must
therefore be subtracted to avoid counting the same
contribution twice~\cite{Diehl:2019rdh,DiehlGauntSchoenwald2017}.
DPS also receives a contribution in which only one of the
two parton pairs arises from a perturbative splitting,
while the other proton supplies two partons without such
a splitting. This contribution has been studied for Higgs
boson pairs~\cite{GauntMaciulaSzczurek2014}.
DPS has also been observed
in same-sign $W$ pair production at the
LHC~\cite{CMS:2022pio,ATLAS:2025dps}. The overlap between single
and double parton scattering has been re-examined using two-loop
splitting kernels~\cite{DiehlPloessl2026}.

In this paper, I expand the two-loop Type~II finite remainders of Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd} in the velocity $\bA$ through $\bA^6$. The main results are the following:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item After the constants that arise in the expansion are expressed in a basis of \BasisCountWord{} transcendental constants, all inverse powers of $\bA$ and the odd powers through $\bA^5$ cancel, and every coefficient is even in $\cos\theta$, as required by Bose symmetry. At leading power, the angle enters only through the logarithm of the transverse momentum $p_T$ of the pseudoscalars.
 \item This logarithm is the DPS singularity. At one loop its coefficient follows the factorised formula of Ref.~\cite{GauntStirling2011} for gluon splitting and the opposite signs of the amplitudes for $g^+g^+\to A$ and $g^-g^-\to A$: with the overall constant fixed at threshold, it reproduces the velocity dependence of the exact one-loop coefficient in the forward limit. A one-loop calculation of $gg\to HH$ confirms the corresponding weight for a CP-even coupling.
 \item At two loops the $J_z=0$ amplitude, with equal gluon helicities, grows as $\ln^3p_T$, and the ratio of the leading logarithms at two and one loops is the one found to all orders in planar $\mathcal N=4$ super Yang--Mills theory~\cite{DixonEsterlis2016}. The $|J_z|=2$ amplitude, with opposite helicities, has no DPS logarithm at one loop but grows as $\ln^2p_T$ at two loops; in the forward limit, this logarithm is the one-loop amplitude multiplied by $-4\Nc\as\ln^2(2p_T/\mA)$ at every velocity tested, and its origin remains open.
 \item In the finite virtual corrections to the cross section,  the one-loop logarithm cancels. At two loops, $\ln^3p_T$ cancels, and the highest power is $\ln^2p_T$.
\end{enumerate}
