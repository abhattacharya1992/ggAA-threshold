\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have expanded the two-loop Type~II finite remainders for $gg\to AA$ about the pair threshold through $\bA^6$ at fixed scattering angle. After the endpoint constants are expressed in a basis of \BasisCountWord{} transcendental constants, all inverse powers of $\bA$ and the odd powers through $\bA^5$ cancel identically, and every coefficient is even in $\cos\theta$. The series reproduce the unexpanded remainders to \PoneErrLePointTwo{} and \PtwoErrLePointTwo{} for $\bA\leq\RecommendedBeta$, including the forward angles tested. They are faster to evaluate than the published expressions and \SizeRatio{} times smaller than the published files. We also found that the released two-loop remainders are larger by a factor of \FileFactor{} than the tree and one-loop amplitudes, and that the released one-loop remainders omit their dependence on the renormalisation scale. With both corrected, the one- and two-loop remainders satisfy the renormalisation group relations exactly.

At leading power the angular dependence enters only through the logarithm of the transverse momentum of the pseudoscalars. We identified this logarithm with the double parton scattering singularity of Gaunt and Stirling. At one loop the velocity dependence of its coefficient follows their factorised formula, with the helicity weights of gluon splitting and the sign structure of a CP-odd coupling, to \DPSMaxDeviation{} over the range of velocities tested, once the overall constant is fixed at threshold. The same identification explains why the $|J_z|=2$ amplitude has no logarithm at one loop, why the coefficient depends neither on $\nf$ nor on $\muR$, and why the logarithm is out of phase with the tree amplitude. At two loops the $J_z=0$ amplitude grows as $\ln^3p_T$ and the $|J_z|=2$ amplitude as $\ln^2p_T$, and the leading logarithms are simple multiples of the one-loop terms. The ratio of the leading logarithms is the one found to all orders in planar $\mathcal N=4$ super Yang--Mills theory, and the next logarithm has the $\beta_0$ structure of the next-to-leading order splitting kernel for $g\to gg$. In the finite virtual corrections to the cross section the logarithm cancels at one loop; at two loops $\ln^3p_T$ cancels and the highest power is $\ln^2p_T$, with coefficient $-\tfrac74\pi^2\Nc^2$.

Several questions remain open. The overall normalisation $\pi\Nc\mA^2$ of the one-loop DPS logarithm should follow from the factorised formula with the effective vertices, and the two-loop logarithms, including those of the $|J_z|=2$ amplitude, from its extension beyond one loop; the evolution equation of planar $\mathcal N=4$ super Yang--Mills theory~\cite{DixonEsterlis2016} and the next-to-leading order splitting kernels of Ref.~\cite{Diehl:2019rdh} suggest the form such an extension might take. If the factorisation carries over to the full theory, the top quark mass changes the normalisation of the DPS logarithm by the on-shell vertex factor of Sec.~\ref{sec:counting}, about \TopMassDPSA{} at $\mA=\TopMassMAA$~GeV; its effect on the remaining terms is not known. On the phenomenological side, the $O_G$--$O_J$ contribution shifts $V_2$ by a constant that remains to be fixed in the present normalisation; two versions of the original code differ in it by \ArchiveOJShift{} in $\as^2V_2$ (App.~\ref{app:normalization}), and the series provide the Type~II two-loop contribution to the hard function for a threshold resummed description of pseudoscalar pairs near $m_{AA}\approx2\mA$. For a pseudoscalar that couples to gluons only through $G\tilde G$, such as an axion-like particle, they give the $O_G$--$O_G$ part of the two-loop virtual corrections at NNLO; combined with the soft and collinear functions of Refs.~\cite{deFlorian:2012za,Bhattacharya:2021nsv}, and once the $O_G$--$O_J$ constant is fixed, they would give a soft-virtual NNLO prediction for the invariant mass distribution of such particles near threshold. The same analysis applies to Higgs pairs produced through two effective vertices, whose one- and two-loop amplitudes are known~\cite{deFlorianMazzitelli2013Virtual,Banerjee:2018lfq}. For a CP-even coupling the amplitudes for $g^+g^+\to H$ and $g^-g^-\to H$ are equal, so the forward logarithm should follow the weight $F_S$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:dps} instead of $F_A$, and the $|J_z|=2$ amplitude should again have no logarithm at one loop.
