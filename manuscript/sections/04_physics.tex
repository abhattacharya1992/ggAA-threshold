\subsection{Leading power}
\label{sec:leading}

To determine the leading threshold behaviour of the
two-loop remainders, I first collect the terms of
order $\bA^0$, including all powers of $\ln\bA$
that multiply them. The notation $\big|_{\bA^0}$ used below
denotes this part of the expansion, rather than
the value of the remainder at $\bA=0$.

In the explicit result, the logarithms of $\bA$
and the angular logarithms combine through
$\ln\bA+\tfrac12\ln(1-c^2)$.
It is convenient to introduce
\begin{align}
 \mathcal L
 &=\ln\bA+\tfrac12\ln(1-c^2)+\ln2+\frac{i\pi}{2}
 \nonumber\\
 &=\tfrac12\ln\bigl[-4\bA^2(1-c^2)\bigr],
 \label{eq:calL}
\end{align}
where the second equality uses the branch $\ln(-X)=\ln X+i\pi$ for $X>0$. As shown below, $\mathcal L$ is the logarithm of the transverse momentum of the pseudoscalars, $\ln(2p_T/\mA)$, up to the constant phase $i\pi/2$ and terms of order $\bA^2$.
%
The relation of $\mathcal L$ to the physical
kinematics follows from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gram-scaling}. Substituting the
expressions for $tu-\mA^4$ and $p_T^2$ gives
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal L
 &=\tfrac12\ln\frac{\mA^4-tu}{\mA^4}
   +\ln(1-\bA^2)
 \\
 &=\ln\frac{2p_T}{\mA}+\frac{i\pi}{2}
   +\tfrac12\ln(1-\bA^2).
\end{align*}
Since $\ln(1-\bA^2)=\Ord(\bA^2)$, the last
term contributes only beyond leading power.
Thus, near threshold, the combination of
velocity and angular logarithms in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:calL} is the logarithm of the
transverse momentum, up to the constant phase
$i\pi/2$ and power corrections.

When the calculated leading power terms are
rewritten in terms of $\mathcal L$, no separate
$\ln(1\pm c)$ or $\Li_2\bigl((1+c)/2\bigr)$
remains. The rational dependence on $c$ also
cancels. The angular dependence is therefore
entirely contained in $\mathcal L$, while the
coefficients of its powers are independent
of the scattering angle. For general $\Nc$
and $\nf$, the result is
\begin{align}
 \Mfull{1}\big|_{\bA^0}
 &=\mA^2\sum_{k=0}^{3}
   \widehat A_{1k}\,\mathcal L^k,
 \nonumber\\
 \Mfull{2}\big|_{\bA^0}
 &=\mA^2\sum_{k=0}^{2}
   \widehat A_{2k}\,\mathcal L^k.
 \label{eq:leading-power}
\end{align}
The highest logarithmic coefficients are
\begin{align}
 \widehat A_{13}&=\LeadOneThree,
 &
 \widehat A_{12}&=\LeadOneTwo,
 \nonumber\\
 \widehat A_{22}&=\LeadTwoTwo,
 &
 \widehat A_{21}&=\LeadTwoOne,
 \label{eq:leading-coefficients}
\end{align}
which do not depend on $\muR$, and
\begin{equation}
 \widehat A_{11}=\LeadOneOnePrefactor\bigl(\Nc\,a_{\mathrm{A}}-\nf\,a_{\mathrm{F}}\bigr),
 \label{eq:M1-L1}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
 a_{\mathrm{A}}&=\LeadOneOneNc,
 \nonumber\\
 a_{\mathrm{F}}&=\LeadOneOneNf,
 \nonumber
\end{align}
and $L_\mu=\ln(\mA^2/\muR^2)$. Table~\ref{tab:leading-log-benchmark} gives all coefficients for $\Nc=\BenchNc$, $\nf=\BenchNf$ and $\muR=\mA$. At $\bA^2$, $\bA^4$ and $\bA^6$ the same substitution again removes every $\ln(1\pm c)$ and $\Li_2$; the remaining dependence on the angle is rational, even in $c$ and free of poles at $c=\pm1$.


Up to the constant $i\pi/2$ and power corrections, $\mathcal L$ is the logarithm of the transverse momentum of the pseudoscalars or, equivalently, of the Gram determinant $tu-\mA^4$. The exact one-loop remainders already contain it, since they depend on $\ln(y-x)+\ln(z-x)$ and $(y-x)(z-x)=yz-1=(tu-\mA^4)/\mA^4$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:xyz-definition}. The two-loop coefficients have no finite limit as $\bA\to0$; they grow as $\mathcal L^3$ and $\mathcal L^2$.

\subsection{One loop}
\label{sec:one-loop}

Fitting the published one-loop remainders at $\bA=$ \OneLoopFitBetas{} with \OneLoopDigits-digit arithmetic, for $\Nc=$ \OneLoopScanNc, $\nf=$ \OneLoopScanNf{}, two values of $\muR$ and several angles, I find to \OneLoopAgreementDigits{} digits
\begin{align}
 \mathcal M_1^{(1)}&=\mA^2\bigl[\OneLoopAone\,\mathcal L+\widehat A^{(1)}_{10}\bigr]+\Ord(\bA),
 \nonumber\\
 \mathcal M_2^{(1)}&=\OneLoopMtwo\,\mA^2+\Ord(\bA),
 \label{eq:one-loop-threshold}
\end{align}
with $\widehat A^{(1)}_{10}=\OneLoopAzero$ for $\Nc=\BenchNc$, $\nf=\BenchNf$ and $\muR=\mA$, in the normalisation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normalisation}. The constant does not depend on the angle, and by Eq.~\eqref{eq:normalisation} its scale dependence is $\partial\widehat A^{(1)}_{10}/\partial\ln\muR^2=4i\beta_0$. The coefficient of $\mathcal L$ and the limit of $\mathcal M_2^{(1)}$ depend neither on $\nf$ nor on $\muR$. Together with Eqs.~\eqref{eq:born} and \eqref{eq:leading-power}, the behaviour at threshold is summarised in Table~\ref{tab:orders}.

\paragraph{Origin of the logarithm.}

The logarithm $\mathcal L$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:one-loop-threshold} can be understood
by examining the Type~II diagrams near $p_T=0$.
In the diagram of Fig.~\ref{fig:diagrams}(b),
each incoming gluon splits into two gluons through
a three-gluon vertex. Gluons from different incoming
partons then fuse pairwise into the pseudoscalars
at the two effective vertices. As $p_T\to0$, all
four internal gluons can become simultaneously
on shell, with two collinear to each incoming
gluon. This configuration is the pinch that
produces the DPS singularity of the
crossed box~\cite{GauntStirling2011}.

The singular contribution can be expressed as
an integral over transverse momentum of the
product of the light cone wavefunctions of the
two incoming gluons and the tree amplitudes of
the two hard subprocesses~\cite{GauntStirling2011}.
The wavefunctions describe the splitting of each
incoming parton. The hard subprocess amplitudes
are evaluated with collinear on-shell incoming
partons and zero transverse momentum. Thus, the
splittings determine the strength of the
singularity, while the hard subprocesses enter
its coefficient. In the present case, the
splittings are $g\to gg$ and the subprocesses
are $gg\to A$ through $O_G$.

For a gluon splitting into a collinear pair,
the daughter helicities add to $0$ or $\pm2$,
whereas the parent helicity is $\pm1$.
The splitting amplitude therefore vanishes in
the exactly collinear limit. For the allowed
tree helicity configurations, its numerator
is linear in the relative transverse momentum.
After including the propagator, the corresponding
light cone wavefunction behaves as the inverse
of that momentum. The transverse momentum
integral is consequently at most logarithmically
singular, as in the massless quark examples
of Ref.~\cite{GauntStirling2011}.

\paragraph{Helicity selection and weights.}

Whether this logarithm is present also depends
on the helicities of the complete singular
configuration. For the fermion loops considered
in Ref.~\cite{GauntStirling2011}, helicity
conservation forces the four intermediate
partons to have $J_z=0$. This restriction does
not hold for general gluon loops. The condition
that remains is conservation of $J_z$ between
the incoming state and the four intermediate
partons~\cite{DixonEsterlis2016}.

For $gg\to AA$, however, each effective vertex
requires the two fusing gluons to have equal
helicities: the amplitude for $gg\to A$ vanishes
for opposite helicities. The gluons in each
fusing pair travel in opposite beam directions,
so equal helicities give $J_z=0$. The four
intermediate gluons therefore have total
$J_z=0$, and a DPS logarithm is possible only
for an incoming state with $J_z=0$. This
explains why the equal helicity coefficient
$\mathcal M_1$ contains a logarithm at one
loop, whereas the opposite helicity coefficient
$\mathcal M_2$ does not.

To determine the dependence of the logarithmic
coefficient on the momentum fractions, consider
an incoming gluon of positive helicity splitting
into gluons $a$ and $b$. Let their longitudinal
momentum fractions be $z$ and $1-z$. With the
coupling, colour and transverse momentum factors
suppressed, the longitudinal weights are
\begin{align}
 w_{++}(z)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{z(1-z)}},
 \nonumber\\
 w_{+-}(z)&=\frac{z^2}{\sqrt{z(1-z)}},
\nonumber\\
 w_{-+}(z)&=\frac{(1-z)^2}{\sqrt{z(1-z)}}.
 \label{eq:splitting-weights}
\end{align}
The subscripts denote the daughter helicities
$(h_a,h_b)$~\cite{Mangano:1990by,Dixon:1996wi}.
These are the longitudinal factors, not the
complete splitting amplitudes. The sum of
their squares is
\begin{equation}
 \dfrac{[1+z^4+(1-z)^4]}{[z(1-z)]},
\end{equation}
which gives the momentum fraction dependence of the
$g\to gg$ splitting function.

At the DPS point, let the first pseudoscalar
receive a fraction $x$ of the first incoming
gluon momentum and a fraction $1-x$ of the
second. The complementary fractions follow
from the equal final state masses, as in
Ref.~\cite{GauntStirling2011}. In
Fig.~\ref{fig:diagrams}(b), this corresponds to
$k=xp_1$ and $p_3-k=(1-x)p_2$.
Here $x$ and $z$ denote longitudinal momentum
fractions, rather than the kinematic variables
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:xyz-definition}.

The CP properties of the effective vertex fix
the relative signs in the helicity sum.
Parity reverses all helicities, and since $A$
is odd under parity, the amplitudes for
$g^+g^+\to A$ and $g^-g^-\to A$ have opposite
signs~\cite{Dixon:1996wi}.
For two positive-helicity incoming gluons,
and with the first helicity of each pair
referring to the gluon that goes to the
first pseudoscalar, the allowed configurations
are those in which both splittings produce
$(+,+)$, both produce $(+,-)$, or both
produce $(-,+)$.
In the first configuration both effective
vertices receive two positive-helicity gluons.
In each mixed configuration, one vertex
receives two positive-helicity gluons and the
other two negative-helicity gluons. The latter
two contributions therefore enter with the
opposite sign. Applying the factorised form of
Ref.~\cite{GauntStirling2011} with these
weights, their sum gives
\begin{align}
 F_A(x)
 &=w_{++}(x)w_{++}(1-x)
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad-w_{+-}(x)w_{+-}(1-x)
       -w_{-+}(x)w_{-+}(1-x)
 \nonumber\\
 &=\frac{1}{x(1-x)}-2x(1-x).
 \label{eq:FA}
\end{align}
Each mixed product contributes $x(1-x)$,
which accounts for the second term.

For a CP-even coupling to $G^2$, the two
subprocess amplitudes have the same sign.
The mixed contributions then add, giving
\begin{equation}
 F_S(x)=\frac{1}{x(1-x)}+2x(1-x).
 \label{eq:FS}
\end{equation}
These weights concern gluon loops with two
effective vertices. They differ from those of
the massless quark boxes for $gg\to HH$
considered in Ref.~\cite{GauntStirling2011}.
For those boxes, the weight is proportional
to $x(1-x)$ because internal quarks of equal
helicity carry complementary momentum
fractions.

\paragraph{Normalisation and the forward limit.}

At threshold, both pseudoscalars are at rest
and $x=\tfrac12$, so that
$F_A(\tfrac12)=\tfrac72$ and
$F_S(\tfrac12)=\tfrac92$.
By Eq.~\eqref{eq:calL}, the coefficient of
$\ln p_T^2$ is half the coefficient of
$\mathcal L$. The one-loop threshold result
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:one-loop-threshold}
therefore gives
\[
 \frac{\OneLoopAone\,\mA^2}{2}
 =\frac72\pi\Nc\mA^2
 =\pi\Nc\mA^2F_A\!\left(\frac12\right).
\]
Matching at threshold thus fixes the overall
factor multiplying $F_A$ to $\pi\Nc\mA^2$.
This factor is obtained from the calculated
amplitude; it has not been derived independently
from the factorisation formula.

Having fixed this constant, the predicted
dependence on $x$ can be tested away from
threshold. The forward limit $c\to1$ at
fixed velocity also gives $p_T\to0$, but the
produced bosons now move along the beam.
Write $\beta$ and $m$ for their velocity and
mass, with $\beta=\bA$ and $m=\mA$ for $AA$.
In this limit, the longitudinal velocity of
the first boson is $2x-1$, so
$x=(1+\beta)/2$.

To extract the logarithmic coefficient, I
differentiate the real part of the one-loop
remainder with respect to $\ln p_T^2$ at
fixed $\beta$ and $m$, and then take the
forward limit. Dividing by the normalisation
fixed above defines
\begin{align}
 D(\beta)
 &\equiv
 \frac{1}{\pi\Nc m^2}
 \lim_{c\to1}
 \frac{\partial\,\Real\mathcal M_1^{(1)}}
      {\partial\ln p_T^2}
 \nonumber\\
 &=F_A\!\left(\frac{1+\beta}{2}\right)
 \qquad (gg\to AA).
 \label{eq:dps-prediction}
\end{align}
The last equality is the prediction to be
tested: no further adjustment of the
normalisation is made as $\beta$ is varied.

I evaluated $D(\beta)$ from the exact one-loop
remainders using $1-c=10^{-14}$ and
$10^{-18}$, over
$\DPSBetaMin\leq\beta\leq\DPSBetaMax$,
with $\Nc=$ \DPSNcValues{} and
$\nf=$ \DPSNfValues{}.
The largest relative deviation from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dps-prediction} was
\DPSMaxDeviation{}.
The extracted coefficients follow the
pseudoscalar weight $F_A$, rather than
the scalar weight $F_S$, over the tested
range.

\paragraph{Comparison with scalar pair production.}

The scalar process provides a separate test
of the relative signs in the helicity sum.
The splittings and colour factors are
unchanged, while replacing the CP-odd
vertices by CP-even ones changes $F_A$
to $F_S$. I tested this prediction using
the one-loop Type~II amplitudes for
$gg\to HH$ at the same mass $m$, computed
with the code of
Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd} after
replacing the effective vertices.
The code reproduces the one-loop $AA$
result of that reference to
\HHReproduceDigits{} digits, and the
$HH$ double poles agree with Catani's
formula~\cite{Catani:1998bh}.

In the conventions used here, the $HH$
tree amplitude at threshold is $-2im^2$,
opposite in sign to the $AA$ amplitude.
The corresponding forward coefficient
satisfies
\[
 D(\beta)
 =-F_S\!\left(\frac{1+\beta}{2}\right)
 \qquad (gg\to HH)
\]
to a relative accuracy of \HHDpsMaxDev{}
for $\HHBetaMin\leq\beta\leq\HHBetaMax$.
The normalisation fixed from $AA$ therefore
also reproduces the $HH$ logarithm without
an additional fitted constant. In units
of the respective tree amplitudes, the
ratio of the scalar and pseudoscalar
logarithmic coefficients at threshold is
\begin{equation}
 \dfrac{F_S(\tfrac12)}{F_A(\tfrac12)} = \dfrac{9}{7}.
\end{equation}
%$F_S(\tfrac12)/F_A(\tfrac12)=\tfrac97$.

The opposite helicity amplitude again
has no logarithm. Its extracted forward
coefficient is below \HHMtwoMax{} times
that of $\mathcal M_1$.
For this check, the diagram containing
the four-gluon term of the CP-even
operator and the contact counterterm
for two such insertions were not
included. Neither depends on $p_T$,
so neither changes the logarithmic
coefficient being tested.

Figure~\ref{fig:dps} compares the extracted coefficients with the two helicity weights. The $AA$ points for $(\Nc,\nf)=(3,5)$ and $(4,2)$ lie on top of each other and on the curve $F_A$, so that after the normalisation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dps-prediction} the coefficient does not depend on $\Nc$ or $\nf$. The $HH$ points (triangles), plotted with their sign reversed, lie on the curve $F_S$, which is separated from $F_A$ by
\begin{equation}
 F_S-F_A=4x(1-x)=1-\beta^2.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Colour, scale dependence and phase.}

The factorisation also explains the flavour
and colour dependence of the one-loop
logarithm. The vertices of $O_G$ couple only
to gluons, so only gluon splittings enter this
singular configuration and its coefficient is
independent of $\nf$. The factor $\Nc$
comes from the colour contraction of
the two three-gluon vertices.
Renormalisation and infrared subtraction
do not change this logarithmic coefficient:
at one loop, their contributions are
proportional to the tree amplitude and
do not generate a logarithm of $p_T$.
The coefficient is therefore independent
of $\muR$.

Finally, the coefficient
$\OneLoopAone\,\mA^2$ of $\mathcal L$ is
real, whereas
$\mathcal M_1^{(0)}\to2i\mA^2$
at threshold. The logarithm is thus
out of phase with the tree amplitude
by $90^\circ$.
This relative phase is consistent
with the absorptive origin of the
one-loop DPS singularity, which can
be obtained from cuts that put
internal lines on
shell~\cite{GauntStirling2011}.
It explains why the one-loop DPS
logarithm does not contribute to
the interference with the tree
amplitude, as discussed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:rate}.

\subsection{Two loops}
\label{sec:two-loop}

The leading logarithms of the calculated two-loop coefficient $\mathcal M_1$ can be written in terms of the one-loop DPS logarithm. Comparing the coefficients of Eq.~\eqref{eq:leading-coefficients} with the coefficient $\OneLoopAone$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:one-loop-threshold} gives, for general $\Nc$ and $\nf$,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat A_{13}\mathcal L^3+\widehat A_{12}\mathcal L^2
 =\OneLoopAone\,\mathcal L\left[\LogRelationCubic\,\mathcal L^2-2\beta_0\,\mathcal L\right],
 \label{eq:log-relation}
\end{equation}
with $\beta_0$ as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normalisation}.
A similar relation holds in planar $\mathcal N=4$ super Yang--Mills theory. Reference~\cite{DixonEsterlis2016} studied the six-gluon maximally helicity violating (MHV) amplitude, which has two negative and four positive gluon helicities when all momenta are taken as outgoing. Near the configuration that mimics double parton scattering, its singular terms obey an evolution equation that determines the leading logarithms to all orders. The ratio of the leading two-loop and one-loop terms, including their powers of the coupling, is $-(a/6)\ln^2\delta$, where $a=g^2\Nc/(8\pi^2)$ and $\delta$ measures the approach to the singular configuration; at fixed values of the other kinematic variables, $\delta$ is proportional to the squared transverse momentum. With $a=2\Nc\as$ and $\ln\delta=2\mathcal L+\mathrm{const}$, the ratio becomes $-\tfrac43\Nc\as\mathcal L^2$ at leading logarithmic order. This agrees with the ratio of the cubic term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:log-relation} to the one-loop logarithm, once the additional power of $\as$ at two loops is included.
The second term, $-2\beta_0\mathcal L$, has no counterpart in the $\mathcal N=4$ theory, whose $\beta$ function vanishes, and the number of quark flavours enters it only through $\beta_0$.

The same $\beta_0$ term appears in the momentum-space kernel for the splitting $g\to gg$ in unpolarised colour singlet double parton distributions at NLO \cite{Diehl:2019rdh}, where the transverse momentum mismatch $\Delta$ between amplitude and conjugate plays the role of $p_T$.
Relative to the LO term, the $\beta_0$ part of the coefficient of $\ln^2(\mu^2/\Delta^2)$, given by Eqs.~(50) and (135) of Ref.~\cite{Diehl:2019rdh} in units of $\alpha_s/2\pi$, corresponds to $-2\beta_0\mathcal L$ in the normalisation of $\as$ used here.
One half comes from the renormalisation of the coupling and the other half from the $\delta$-function parts of the DGLAP kernels, so that the running of the coupling alone would give $-\beta_0\mathcal L$. The remaining terms, one proportional to $C_A\ln[x(1-x)]$ from the plus distributions, which contributes where the two momentum fractions add up to one, and the real emission terms, which contribute where they add up to less than one, have no counterpart in the amplitude.

The $|J_z|=2$ amplitude acquires a logarithm at two loops, $\widehat A_{22}=(\MtwoRelation)\cdot\OneLoopMtwo$, that is the one-loop constant multiplied by $\MtwoRelation\,\mathcal L^2$. The $J_z$ argument of Sec.~\ref{sec:dps} concerns the one-loop singularity and does not forbid this. The logarithm cannot come from two-loop amplitudes of the subprocesses, since conservation of angular momentum forbids $gg\to A$ for opposite gluon helicities to all orders, so the one-loop mechanism does not explain it; I leave its origin open. A related effect occurs in planar $\mathcal N=4$ super Yang--Mills theory: the next-to-MHV (NMHV) six-gluon amplitude, with three negative helicities, has no such singularity at one loop because of $J_z$ conservation, but has logarithmic singularities from two loops on, found where the limit overlaps with multi-Regge kinematics; Dixon and Esterlis suggest that they may come from contributions in which more than one particle crosses a cut~\cite{DixonEsterlis2016}.

Figure~\ref{fig:amplitudes} compares the three orders. With $\as=\BenchAs$ ($\alpha_s=\BenchAlphaS$), at $\bA=\LoopSizeBeta$ and $c=\BenchCos$, the one and two-loop terms of $\mathcal M_1$ are \MoneOneLoopRatio{} and \MoneTwoLoopRatio{} times the tree term, and the one-loop term of $\mathcal M_2$ is \MtwoOneLoopRatio{} times the tree term. The last ratio grows as $\bA^{-2}$ towards threshold, since the tree-level $|J_z|=2$ amplitude vanishes there. The one-loop terms include the scale term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normalisation}. Towards threshold the loop terms of $\mathcal M_1$ grow with $|\mathcal L|$, the two-loop term as $|\mathcal L|^3$ and the one-loop term as $|\mathcal L|$, so that below $\bA\approx\CrossoverBeta$ the two-loop term exceeds the one-loop term at this angle. Because the logarithms are out of phase with the tree amplitude, this growth largely drops out of the cross section (Sec.~\ref{sec:rate}). In panel (b) the one-loop term of $\mathcal M_2$ tends to the constant of Eq.~\eqref{eq:one-loop-threshold}, while the two-loop term grows as $\mathcal L^2$, the logarithm that the $|J_z|=2$ amplitude acquires at two loops.
%
