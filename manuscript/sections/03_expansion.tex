\section{Threshold expansion}
\label{sec:expansion}

The calculation has four steps, each checked independently. First, the finite remainders of Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd} are split into their flavour coefficients and brought to the normalisation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normalisation} (App.~\ref{app:normalization}). Second, each flavour coefficient is expanded about $\bA=0$ at fixed angle, through $\bA^4$ and, independently, through $\bA^6$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:construction}). Third, the constants that arise at the endpoints are reduced to a basis of \BasisCountWord{} transcendental constants, after which the inverse and odd powers of $\bA$ cancel identically (Sec.~\ref{sec:cancellations} and App.~\ref{app:constants}). Fourth, the series are compared with the unexpanded remainders, evaluated in Mathematica at increasing working precision and, at selected points, with an independent Python code (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).  The software and the computing environment are described in App.~\ref{app:data}.

\subsection{Construction}
\label{sec:construction}

The remainders of Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd} are functions of $x$, $y$ and $z$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:xyz-definition}). We replace these variables by their expressions in $\bA$ and $c$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:threshold-map}, and expand each flavour coefficient about $\bA=0$ at fixed $c$,
\begin{equation}
 \Mflav{i}{r}(\bA,c)
 =\sum_{n=-4}^{p}\sum_k
 C^{(i,r)}_{nk}\,\bA^n\ln^k\bA
 +\mathcal O_{\log}(\bA^{p+1}),
 \label{eq:series-structure}
\end{equation}
with $r=0,1,2$ and $p=4$ or 6. The coefficients $C^{(i,r)}_{nk}$ depend on $c$, $\mA^2$, $\muR^2$ and $\Nc$, and $\mathcal O_{\log}(\bA^n)$ stands for $\bA^n$ times a polynomial in $\ln\bA$. Inverse powers appear in intermediate steps because the tensor basis and the rational prefactors contain inverse Gram determinants, Eq.~\eqref{eq:gram-scaling}. They must be kept until all contributions at a given power have been combined.

The remainders contain logarithms, classical polylogarithms and the function $\Li_{2,2}$ of weight four~\cite{Frellesvig:2016ske,Vollinga:2004sn}, whose arguments approach correlated endpoints as $\bA\to0$. We expand the rational prefactors exactly and obtain the expansions of the special functions from differential equations in $\bA$, with boundary values at $\bA=0$. These equations have the iterated-integral form familiar from harmonic polylogarithms~\cite{RemiddiVermaseren2000} and from canonical differential equations for Feynman integrals~\cite{Henn2013}; the map $x=(1-\bA)/(1+\bA)$ is one of the transformations under which harmonic polylogarithms are closed~\cite{RemiddiVermaseren2000}.
The method thus expands the known remainders, not the loop integrals, which could instead be expanded near threshold by the method of regions~\cite{BenekeSmirnov1998}. Expansions of two-loop gluon fusion amplitudes in kinematic limits are also used to obtain compact representations that are fast to evaluate, for example for small transverse momentum and at high energy~\cite{Bellafronte:2022,Davies:2023} and about the top quark pair threshold~\cite{Groeber:2018}; the present expansion is about the pair production threshold of the massless remainders.
For the dilogarithm, for example, $\dd\Li_2(x)/\dd\bA=2(1-\bA^2)^{-1}\ln[2\bA/(1+\bA)]$ with $\Li_2(1)=\pi^2/6$ gives
\begin{align}
 \Li_2(x)
 &=
 \frac{\pi^2}{6}
 +2\bA\bigl[\ln(2\bA)-1\bigr]
 -\bA^2
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad
 +\bA^3
 \left[
   \frac{2}{3}\ln(2\bA)
   +\frac{1}{9}
 \right]
 -\frac{2}{3}\bA^4
 +\Ord\!\left(\bA^5\ln\bA\right).
 \label{eq:li2-threshold-example}
\end{align}
Since the prefactors start at $\bA^{-4}$, the special functions have to be expanded four orders beyond the final accuracy. The fourth and sixth order expansions were constructed independently, and all coefficients common to both agree.

\subsection{Endpoint constants and exact cancellations}
\label{sec:cancellations}

The boundary values at $\bA=0$ involve \ConstantCountWord{} constants
$\mathrm{MB}[p,q,a,w]$, defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mb-definition}
of App.~\ref{app:constants}, with $a,w\in\{-1,\tfrac12,1\}$.
Using the PSLQ algorithm~\cite{Ferguson:1999} at \PSLQDigits{} digits,
we identify integer relations expressing these constants in the basis
\begin{equation}
 \{\zeta_3,\ \pi^2\ln2,\ \ln^32,\ \pi^4,\ \pi^2\ln^22,\
 \ln^42,\ \Li_4(\tfrac12),\ \zeta_3\ln2\},
 \label{eq:constant-basis}
\end{equation}
whose elements have weight three or four and are of the type
encountered in alternating Euler sums~\cite{Blumlein:2009cf}.
The numerically identified relations are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:mb-constants}; the largest residual is \PSLQResidual{}.

After substituting these relations, we use the reflection and Landen
identities to express the dilogarithms with arguments $(1-c)/2$,
$(c-1)/(c+1)$ and $(c+1)/(c-1)$ in terms of
$\Li_2\bigl((1+c)/2\bigr)$.
With the overall factor $\mA^2$ extracted, the coefficient of each
power $\bA^n$ is a polynomial in
\begin{equation}
 \bigl\{\ln\bA,\ \ln(1\pm c),\
 \Li_2\bigl(\tfrac{1+c}{2}\bigr),\
 \ln\tfrac{\mA^2}{\muR^2}\bigr\}
 \cup\{\ln2,\ \pi,\ \zeta_3,\ \Li_4(\tfrac12)\},
 \label{eq:function-basis}
\end{equation}
with coefficients rational in $c$ and $\Nc$.
Treating the listed quantities as algebraically independent,
we verify symbolically that
\begin{enumerate}
 \item the coefficients of $\bA^{-4},\dots,\bA^{-1}$ vanish
 identically in every flavour coefficient;
 \item the coefficients of $\bA$, $\bA^3$ and $\bA^5$ vanish
 identically, so that through $\bA^6$ the expansion is a series
 in $\bA^2$ with logarithms;
 \item the \LeadCheckedCount{} coefficients at orders $\bA^0$,
 $\bA^2$, $\bA^4$ and $\bA^6$ across the six flavour components
 are even in $c$, so that Bose symmetry holds order by order.
\end{enumerate}
%
The cancellations and the reductions of the constants were confirmed independently in Mathematica.


The cancellations have a simple origin. Exchanging the pseudoscalars
exchanges $t$ and $u$, so the remainders depend on the kinematics through
invariants that are symmetric under this exchange,
\begin{equation}
 \hat s=\frac{4\mA^2}{1-\bA^2},\qquad
 (t-u)^2=\hat s^2\bA^2c^2,\qquad
 tu-\mA^4=\hat s\,p_T^2,
 \label{eq:symmetric-invariants}
\end{equation}
with $p_T^2$ given in Eq.~\eqref{eq:gram-scaling}. The difference
$t-u=\hat s\bA c$, which is odd in $\bA$, enters only through its square.
Each invariant depends on $\bA$ only through $\bA^2$ and on $c$ only
through $c^2$, so a function analytic in them at threshold is a series in
$\bA^2$ and even in $c$. Odd or inverse powers of $\bA$ would therefore signal
non-analytic behaviour at threshold. There is no threshold for a pair of
massive internal particles at $\hat s=4\mA^2$, since the loops contain no
massive particles, and the colourless final state has no QCD Coulomb
singularity. These observations alone do not exclude other Landau
singularities: the singularity at $p_T\to0$ discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:dps}
is one, and at one loop it is logarithmic~\cite{GauntStirling2011}. The Gram
determinant $tu-\mA^4$ vanishes at the same point, but its inverse powers come
from the tensor basis and the rational prefactors; they are not singularities
of the amplitude. In the present calculation, the explicit expansion shows
that the inverse powers cancel and that the remaining singular behaviour is
logarithmic in $p_T$.
A remainder of the form $\sum_k a_k\ln^k p_T^2$, with coefficients $a_k$
analytic in the invariants of Eq.~\eqref{eq:symmetric-invariants} at
threshold, is therefore a series in $\bA^2$ with logarithms and has no
inverse powers. This accounts for the form of the cancellations found above.
At two loops it is not an independent proof, because there the logarithmic
nature of the singularity is established only by the calculation itself.

Table~\ref{tab:leading-threshold-inventory} lists the leading terms. Since
the odd powers vanish, the first omitted term is expected to be
$\Ord_{\log}(\bA^{p+2})$ rather than the $\Ord_{\log}(\bA^{p+1})$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:series-structure}. The truncation errors should therefore
scale as $\bA^6$ for $p=4$ and as $\bA^8$ for $p=6$, up to logarithmic
factors.

The coefficient of $\nf^2$ is the simplest:
\begin{align}
 \Mflav{1}{2}(\bA,c)&=K+\Ord(\bA^2),
 \label{eq:nf2-leading-one}\\
 \Mflav{2}{2}(\bA,c)&=\bA^2(1-c^2)K+\Ord(\bA^4),
 \label{eq:nf2-leading-two}
\end{align}
where $K$ does not depend on the angle. It is the only flavour coefficient that follows the tree-level pattern $\mathcal M_2\propto\bA^2(1-c^2)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:born}, with the same ratio to the tree amplitude in both tensor structures. The complete expressions through $\bA^6$ are released as Wolfram Language files (App.~\ref{app:data}).

\subsection{Accuracy}
\label{sec:validation}

We compared the series with the unexpanded remainders on
the grid in Table~\ref{tab:validation-grid}, which contains
\BetaPointCount{} values of $\bA$ between \ValBetaMin{}
and $\ValBetaMax$, \AnglePointCount{} scattering angles
and \ParameterSetCount{} combinations of mass,
renormalisation scale and number of colours.
At each of the \GridPointCount{} points, the comparison
included the six flavour coefficients and the two complete
coefficients, giving \ValidationRows{} comparisons in total.
Each unexpanded remainder was evaluated with 80, 120, 160,
220 and 300 digits of working precision, and a value was
accepted once two successive precisions agreed to a relative
difference below $10^{-25}$, with at least 30 significant
digits in both. The parameter set with $\Nc\neq3$ was
included to test the colour dependence; all physical
results use $\Nc=\BenchNc$.
The relative truncation error is defined as
\begin{equation}
 E_i^{(p)}
 =
 \frac{\left|\mathcal M_i^{[p]}-\mathcal M_i\right|}
      {\left|\mathcal M_i\right|},
 \qquad p=4,6,
 \label{eq:relative-error}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal M_i$ is the unexpanded remainder and $\mathcal M_i^{[p]}$ its series through $\bA^p$.

Figure~\ref{fig:validation} shows these errors for the complete coefficients
at $\nf=\BenchNf$. The slopes of the curves, discussed below, test the
scaling $\bA^{p+2}$ expected from the cancellation of the odd powers.
Because each curve is a maximum over the points of
Table~\ref{tab:validation-grid}, a change of the point that gives the
maximum as $\bA$ varies would distort these slopes. For the series through
$\bA^6$ this does not occur: for both coefficients and at every velocity
the maximum comes from the same point, $c=\ValMaxCos$ with $\mA=\ValMaxMA$~GeV,
$\muR=\ValMaxMuR$~GeV and $\Nc=\ValMaxNc$, so the curves follow the error of a single point.
For the series through $\bA^4$ the same holds for $\mathcal M_1$, while for $\mathcal M_2$ the maximum alternates between two parameter sets at $|c|=$\ValAltCos.
The maximum is also representative of the grid: at each velocity and
for both orders the errors at all other points lie within a factor of \ValSpreadWord{}
of it.

For the series through $\bA^4$, the fitted slopes of the logarithm of the maximum error against $\ln\bA$ over the plotted range are \PoneSlopeFour{}
for $\mathcal M_1$ and \PtwoSlopeFour{} for $\mathcal M_2$; for the series through $\bA^6$ they are \PoneSlopeSix{} and \PtwoSlopeSix.
The expected values are 6 and 8 (Sec.~\ref{sec:cancellations}), while a surviving odd power would give values close to 5 and 7. The small deviations from 6 and 8 are consistent with the powers of $\ln\bA$ and the higher terms omitted from the series.

In the lower panels, exact power behaviour would give horizontal lines.
The scaled errors vary slowly, because both the omitted terms and the remainders that normalise the errors contain powers of $\ln\bA$, and over the tested range they change by less than a factor of two.
An incorrect coefficient at a retained order would instead make them grow as an inverse
power of $\bA$ as $\bA$ decreases. Towards $\bA=\EdgeBeta$ the scaled errors of the series through $\bA^6$ rise for both coefficients, as the terms beyond the first omitted order begin to contribute.


Table~\ref{tab:AA-threshold-accuracy} lists the maximum errors at selected velocities. For $\bA\leq\RecommendedBeta$, the series through $\bA^6$ reproduces $\mathcal M_1$ and $\mathcal M_2$ with relative errors of at most \PoneErrLePointTwo{} and \PtwoErrLePointTwo{}; at $\bA=\EdgeBeta$ the errors are \PoneErrAtPointThree{} and \PtwoErrAtPointThree{}.
Bose symmetry was checked by comparing the values at $c$ and $-c$: they \BoseStatement{} in \AngularReferenceChecks{} comparisons of the unexpanded coefficients and \AngularSeriesChecks{} of the series.
The reference values were computed in Mathematica from the expressions of Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd}. To check their evaluation independently, we wrote a separate Python code with mpmath~\cite{mpmath}. It reads the published files with its own parser and computes $\Li_{2,2}$ from its defining double sum, or from a one-dimensional integral representation where the sum converges slowly. At \ExactPointCount{} points covering the \ExactParameterSets{} parameter sets, the two complete coefficients agree with the reference values to a relative difference of at most \ExactMaxComplete{}, the precision to which the reference values are stored. The \ExactComponentCount{} flavour coefficients, compared at $\bA=\ExactComponentBeta$ and $c=\ExactComponentCos$, agree to \ExactMaxComponent{}. At $c=0$, where $t=u$, the published expressions have removable singularities and are evaluated as limits.

The expansion is made at fixed angle, but $p_T$ vanishes as $c\to\pm1$ at fixed $\bA$, so the series must also be tested close to the beam axis. We therefore compared it with the unexpanded remainders at $c=$ \ForwardCosList{} for $\bA=$ \ForwardBetaList{}. Table~\ref{tab:forward-angles} gives the relative errors of the series through $\bA^6$. \ForwardSummary{} The series thus remains accurate close to the beam, consistent with its coefficients having no poles at $c=\pm1$, only logarithms of $1\pm c$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:leading}).
