\section{Flavour decomposition}
\label{app:flavour}

The decomposition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:nf-decomposition} separates the part of
each remainder that is independent of $\nf$ from the parts proportional to
$\nf$ and $\nf^2$. To see whether these parts add or cancel in the complete
remainder, we evaluate it at $\nf=\BenchNf$:
\begin{align}
 \Mfull{i}(\BenchNf)
 &=\Mflav{i}{0}
   +\BenchNf\,\Mflav{i}{1}
   +\BenchNf^2\,\Mflav{i}{2}
 \nonumber\\
 &\equiv a_0+a_1+a_2,
 \qquad i=1,2.
 \label{eq:nf5-sum}
\end{align}
The index $i$ is suppressed in $a_r$. The factors $\BenchNf$ and
$\BenchNf^2$ are included in $a_1$ and $a_2$, so these are the three terms
of the complete remainder rather than the flavour coefficients themselves.

Figure~\ref{fig:flavour-components} shows $|a_r|/|\Mfull{i}(\BenchNf)|$ at
$c=\BenchCos$, $\Nc=\BenchNc$ and $\mA=\muR=\BenchMA\,\mathrm{GeV}$.
Since the terms are complex, the ratios need not add to unity;
a value above unity means that a term is larger in magnitude than the full remainder and is partly cancelled by the others.
\begin{figure}[!ht]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig2_flavour.pdf}
 \caption{Magnitudes of the three terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:nf5-sum}, divided by the magnitude of the complete coefficient: (a) $\mathcal M_1$, (b) $\mathcal M_2$, for $c=\BenchCos$, $\mA=\muR=\BenchMA\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and $\Nc=\BenchNc$. The horizontal line marks unity.}
 \label{fig:flavour-components}
\end{figure}
The $\nf$ independent term is the largest in both coefficients over the plotted range.
The term linear in $\nf$ is not small, and for
$\mathcal M_1$, it partly cancels the $\nf^0$ term. The cancellation among the three terms can be measured by
\[
 1-\frac{|a_0+a_1+a_2|}
          {|a_0|+|a_1|+|a_2|},
\]
which vanishes when the terms have the same phase and approaches one when
they cancel completely. For $\mathcal M_1$, it lies between
\PoneCancellationMin{} and \PoneCancellationMax{} over the velocities shown,
so the complete remainder is roughly half the sum of the magnitudes of its
parts. This refers to the remainder, not the cross section. The flavour
terms are therefore not a small correction, and a remainder evaluated at
one value of $\nf$ cannot be rescaled to another.
The relative signs can be seen in the leading power coefficients.
In $\widehat A_{12}$,  the flavour dependence enters through
$\beta_0=(11\Nc-2\nf)/3$, so there the quark and gluon contributions offset
each other as in the running of the coupling; $\widehat A_{11}$ and
$\widehat A_{21}$ contain $\Nc a_{\mathrm A}-\nf a_{\mathrm F}$ and
$4\Nc-\nf$ [Eqs.~\eqref{eq:leading-coefficients} and
\eqref{eq:M1-L1}]. These combinations show the relative minus signs; the
size of the cancellation in the complete remainder also depends on their
phases and on the powers of $\mathcal L$ that they multiply.

Finally, the $\nf^2$ term of $\mathcal M_2$ starts at $\bA^2(1-c^2)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:nf2-leading-two}).
At leading power, this term is proportional to the tree amplitude, with the same factor in both tensor structures (Sec.~\ref{sec:cancellations}), so in $\mathcal M_2$,  it shares
the centrifugal suppression of the $|J_z|=2$ tree amplitude.
This is why its relative contribution in panel~(b) becomes small near threshold.
