\section{Consequences for the cross section}
\label{sec:rate}

\subsection{Leading order}

Averaging Eq.~\eqref{eq:helicity} over helicities and colours, and including a factor $\tfrac12$ for identical particles, gives the LO partonic cross section of the Type~II amplitude,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd\hat\sigma}{\dd c}
 =\frac{\bA\,C_G^4}{128\pi\hat s\,(\Nc^2-1)}\left(|\mathcal M_1^{(0)}|^2+|\mathcal M_2^{(0)}|^2\right)
 \label{eq:dsigma}
\end{equation}
for $-1<c<1$. For $\alpha_s=\BenchAlphaS$, $\cot\beta=\BenchCotBeta$ and $\mA=\BenchMA\,\mathrm{GeV}$ I find $\hat\sigma=\SigmaLO\,\mathrm{pb}$ at $\bA=\SigmaBeta$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:cross-section}(a)). The cross section rises as $\bA$ above threshold and as $\hat s$ at large $\hat s$, as expected for dimension-five operators, and it is of fourth order in $\as$ through $C_G^4$. In a 2HDM, it is a small part of the total, which is dominated by Type~I (Sec.~\ref{sec:counting}).
%

 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/fig6_cross_section.pdf}
 \caption{(a) Leading order partonic cross section of the Type~II amplitude, Eq.~\eqref{eq:dsigma}, for $\mA=\BenchMA\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $\alpha_s=\BenchAlphaS$ and $\cot\beta=\BenchCotBeta$. (b) Angle averages $\as\langle V_1\rangle$ and $\as^2\langle V_2\rangle$ of the finite virtual corrections, Eq.~\eqref{eq:angle-average}, for $\muR=\mA$, $\Nc=\BenchNc$ and $\nf=\BenchNf$.}

\end{figure*}

\subsection{Finite virtual corrections}

To examine the effect of the loop corrections on the Type~II squared amplitude, I define two dimensionless
coefficients, $V_1$ and $V_2$, relative to the tree contribution. Let $\mathcal A_{\Fin}$ denote the
amplitude constructed from the finite remainders of Eq.~\eqref{eq:catani}, in the normalisation of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:normalisation}. Using the helicity decomposition in Eq.~\eqref{eq:helicity}, its square
can be written as
\begin{align}
 \sum_{\mathrm{hel,col}}|\mathcal A_{\Fin}|^2
 &=2(\Nc^2-1)\,C_G^4
   \sum_i|\mathcal M_i^{(0)}|^2
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad\times
 \left[1+\as V_1+\as^2V_2+\Ord(\as^3)\right], \text{ where }
 \nonumber\\
 V_1
 &=\frac{
  2\Real\sum_i
  \mathcal M_i^{(0)*}\mathcal M_i^{(1)}
 }{
  \sum_i|\mathcal M_i^{(0)}|^2
 },
 \nonumber\\
 V_2
 &=\frac{
  \sum_i\left(
   2\Real\bigl[\mathcal M_i^{(0)*}\Mfull{i}\bigr]
   +|\mathcal M_i^{(1)}|^2
  \right)
 }{
  \sum_i|\mathcal M_i^{(0)}|^2
 }.
 \label{eq:virtual}
\end{align}
The sums run over the two tensor coefficients, $i=1,2$. The coefficient $V_1$ contains the
interference between the tree and one-loop amplitudes. At the next order, $V_2$ contains
both the interference between the tree and two-loop amplitudes and the square of the
one-loop amplitude.
%s
These coefficients refer only to the $O_G$--$O_G$ Type~II contribution. Their
interpretation as NLO and NNLO finite virtual terms applies when the Type~II
tree amplitude is the leading amplitude, as for a pseudoscalar coupled to gluons
only through $G\tilde G$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:counting}). They do not
include interference with the Type~I amplitudes in a 2HDM.
%
The real emission contributions are not included here. The quantities
$V_1$ and $V_2$ therefore retain the dependence on the subtraction scheme
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:catani}, which cancels only when the real emission and the
counterterms for initial state collinear factorisation are added; they are
not observables or $K$ factors. The coefficient of $\ell^2$ derived below
is not affected by finite changes of the subtraction scheme.

At fixed $\bA$, both coefficients depend on the scattering angle. To display
their velocity dependence in Fig.~\ref{fig:cross-section}(b), I average them
over the angle with the Type~II LO cross section as the weight. Since the
remaining factors in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dsigma} depend only on $\bA$ and cancel
in this average, $\as\langle V_1\rangle$ and $\as^2\langle V_2\rangle$ are
the finite virtual contributions relative to the angle-integrated tree
contribution, rather than unweighted averages. The weighted average is
\begin{equation}
 \langle V_n\rangle
 =
 \frac{
  \displaystyle
  \int_{-1}^{1}\dd c\,
  \sum_i|\mathcal M_i^{(0)}|^2\,V_n
 }{
  \displaystyle
  \int_{-1}^{1}\dd c\,
  \sum_i|\mathcal M_i^{(0)}|^2
 },
 \qquad n=1,2.
 \label{eq:angle-average}
\end{equation}

I evaluate the one-loop remainders without a threshold expansion and the
two-loop remainders with the series through $\bA^6$. For
$\alpha_s=\BenchAlphaS$ and $\muR=\mA=\BenchMA\,\mathrm{GeV}$, the curves of
Fig.~\ref{fig:cross-section}(b) lie between \VoneMin{} and \VoneMax{} for
$\as\langle V_1\rangle$, and between \VtwoMin{} and \VtwoMax{} for
$\as^2\langle V_2\rangle$, over $\VirtualBetaMin\leq\bA\leq\VirtualBetaMax$.
At fixed kinematics, the scale dependence of the one-loop remainder in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:normalisation} gives $\partial V_1/\partial\ln\muR^2=4\beta_0$.

\paragraph{One-loop contribution.}

The threshold behaviour of $V_1$ and $V_2$ follows from the relative phases
of the tree and loop amplitudes. At fixed scattering angle,
$\mathcal M_1^{(0)}\to2i\mA^2$, whereas $\mathcal M_2^{(0)}$ vanishes as
$\bA^2$. The squared tree amplitude in the denominator of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:virtual} therefore tends to $4\mA^4$, and at leading power
interference with the tree amplitude selects the imaginary part of
$\mathcal M_1^{(1)}$ or $\Mfull{1}$. With $\ell=\ln(2p_T/\mA)$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:calL} gives $\mathcal L=\ell+i\pi/2+\Ord(\bA^2)$, and the
one-loop result of Eq.~\eqref{eq:one-loop-threshold} reads
\[
 \frac{\mathcal M_1^{(1)}}{\mA^2}
 =
 \OneLoopAone\,\ell
 +\frac{i\pi}{2}\OneLoopAone
 +\widehat A^{(1)}_{10}.
\]
The coefficient of $\ell$ is real, so the logarithm drops out of the
interference with the imaginary tree amplitude. Only the imaginary
constants remain:
\begin{equation}
 V_1\longrightarrow
 \frac{\pi}{2}\OneLoopAone
 +\Imag\widehat A^{(1)}_{10}.
 \label{eq:v1-threshold}
\end{equation}
For $\Nc=\BenchNc$, $\nf=\BenchNf$ and $\muR=\mA$, this gives
$V_1\to\VoneThreshold$. The one-loop remainder thus contains the DPS
logarithm, but the leading power finite virtual correction does not. The
limit is also independent of the angle, which is why the angular average in
Fig.~\ref{fig:cross-section}(b) is nearly constant.

\paragraph{Two-loop contribution.}

The interference of the two-loop amplitude with the tree amplitude again
selects the imaginary part of the loop remainder, but the square of the
one-loop amplitude retains the real logarithm. Using
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:leading-power} and \eqref{eq:one-loop-threshold}, the leading
power terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:virtual} are
\begin{align}
 V_2\big|_{\bA^0}
 &=
 \Imag\left[
  \sum_{k=0}^{3}\widehat A_{1k}\mathcal L^k
 \right] +
 \frac14\left|
  \OneLoopAone\,\mathcal L
  +\widehat A^{(1)}_{10}
 \right|^2
 +\frac{\pi^2\Nc^2}{16}.
\end{align}
The three terms are the interference of the two-loop amplitude with the
tree amplitude, the square of $\mathcal M_1^{(1)}$, and the square of
$\mathcal M_2^{(1)}\to\pi\Nc\mA^2/2$. The last contributes at leading power
although the corresponding tree amplitude vanishes, while the interference
of $\mathcal M_2^{(0)}$ with $\Mfull{2}$ is suppressed by $\bA^2$, up to
logarithms.

The coefficients $\widehat A_{13}$ and $\widehat A_{12}$ are real, and at
leading power
\[
 \Imag\mathcal L^3
 =\frac{3\pi}{2}\ell^2-\frac{\pi^3}{8},
 \qquad
 \Imag\mathcal L^2=\pi\ell.
\]
The cubic logarithm of the two-loop amplitude therefore gives only a
quadratic logarithm in its interference with the tree amplitude, and
$\widehat A_{12}\mathcal L^2$ only a linear one. The square of the one-loop
amplitude is at most quadratic in $\ell$. The leading threshold expansion
of $V_2$ therefore has the form
\begin{equation}
 V_2=v_0+v_1\ell+v_2\ell^2
     +\Ord_{\log}(\bA^2),
 \label{eq:v2-form}
\end{equation}
where the subscripts of $v_0$, $v_1$ and $v_2$ give the power of $\ell$,
not the number of loops.

The coefficient $v_2$ receives two contributions: $\tfrac32\pi\widehat
A_{13}=-14\pi^2\Nc^2$ from the interference of the two-loop amplitude with
the tree amplitude, and $\tfrac14(\OneLoopAone)^2=\tfrac{49}{4}\pi^2\Nc^2$
from the square of the one-loop amplitude. Their sum is
\begin{equation}
 v_2
 =-14\pi^2\Nc^2+\frac{49}{4}\pi^2\Nc^2
 =-\frac74\pi^2\Nc^2.
 \label{eq:v2-log}
\end{equation}
The two contributions partly cancel. At leading power, the DPS logarithm
therefore first appears in the finite virtual correction in $V_2$, with
highest power $\ell^2$.

A fit of the numerical values of $V_2$ at $c=\BenchCos$ over
$\VtwoBetaSmall\leq\bA\leq\VtwoBetaLarge$ gives $v_2=\VtwoLogFit$, while
Eq.~\eqref{eq:v2-log} gives $\VtwoLogExact$ for $\Nc=\BenchNc$; the
relative difference before rounding is \VtwoLogRelDiff{}. For
$\as=\BenchAs$, the quadratic term in the finite virtual correction is
$\as^2v_2\ell^2=\VtwoLogScaled\,\ell^2$.


\paragraph{Remaining coefficients and numerical behaviour.}

At leading power, $v_0$, $v_1$ and $v_2$ are independent of the scattering
angle. The angular dependence of $V_2$ itself remains through $\ell$.
Fits for $v_0$ and $v_1$ at $c=0$, $\tfrac13$ and $\tfrac23$
agree within a relative spread of \VtwoAngleSpread{}.
The coefficient of the linear logarithm follows from the same expansion:
\begin{align*}
 v_1
 &=\pi\widehat A_{12}
   +\Imag\widehat A_{11}
   +\frac{\OneLoopAone}{2}
    \Real\widehat A^{(1)}_{10}
 \\
 &=\frac72\pi\Nc\Real\widehat A^{(1)}_{10}
   +7\pi^2\Nc\beta_0.
\end{align*}
For the benchmark parameters, this gives $v_1=\VoneFit$, in agreement with
the numerical fit to a relative difference of \VoneFormulaDev{}.
The coefficient is independent of $\muR$, since
$\Real\widehat A^{(1)}_{10}$ is scale independent. The same is true
of $v_2$. At leading power, the explicit scale dependence of $V_2$
is therefore confined to $v_0$, which changes from \VzeroBench{}
to \VzeroTwoMu{} when $\muR$ is increased from $\mA$ to $2\mA$.

Although $\ell^2$ is the highest power of the logarithm, it need
not give the largest contribution at the velocities considered.
At $c=\BenchCos$, the ratio $(v_1\ell+v_2\ell^2)/V_2$ is
approximately \LogShareLarge\% at $\bA=\LogShareBetaLarge$
and \LogShareSmall\% at $\bA=\LogShareBetaSmall$.
The quadratic term alone contributes $-\SquareShareLarge\%$ and
$-\SquareShareSmall\%$ of the total $V_2$, respectively.
Since $\ell<0$ in this region and both $v_1$ and $v_2$ are
negative, the linear term is positive and the quadratic term is negative.
%
This competition also explains the shape of the correction as a
function of velocity. The leading power expression has a maximum at
\[
 \ell_{\mathrm{max}}
 =-\frac{v_1}{2v_2}
 =\VtwoPeakEll.
\]
At $c=\BenchCos$, this corresponds to $\bA\simeq\VtwoPeakBeta$.
For larger $\bA$, $\ell$ increases and the leading power value of
$V_2$ decreases. The same balance between the linear and quadratic
terms explains the turnover of the angular average
$\as^2\langle V_2\rangle$ in Fig.~\ref{fig:cross-section}(b).
At $c=\BenchCos$ and $\bA=\LogShareBetaLarge$,
including the terms beyond leading power changes $V_2$ by only \LeadPowerDevLarge\%.

\paragraph{DPS interpretation and subtraction dependence.}

The two contributions to $v_2$ also differ in their relation to the DPS
configuration. In the square of the one-loop amplitude, the DPS
region occurs in both the amplitude and its complex conjugate, that is,
on both sides of the final state cut. This is the configuration
associated with perturbative splittings in both protons that
overlaps with DPS and requires a subtraction to avoid double
counting~\cite{DiehlGauntSchoenwald2017,DiehlPloessl2026}.
In the interference with the tree amplitude, the DPS region occurs
only on the loop side of the cut. Both contributions enter the
finite virtual coefficient $V_2$ defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:virtual}; the
logarithmic term in its one-loop square alone would therefore
not give the complete coefficient $v_2$.

Although $V_2$ depends on the IR subtraction convention, $v_2$ is unchanged by finite
scheme changes that multiply lower order amplitudes by angle independent functions
regular at the pair threshold. The tree amplitude has no
logarithm, and the one-loop remainder contains at most one power of $\ell$. Such changes can therefore
affect the constant and linear terms in $V_2$, but not the coefficient of $\ell^2$.

Finally, with the Wilson coefficient counting specified in
Sec.~\ref{sec:counting}, the one-loop $O_G$--$O_J$ amplitude contributes
at the same order as the two-loop $O_G$--$O_G$ amplitude.
Its coefficient $C_J$ depends on $m_t$ in the heavy top quark
theory~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd}. By Eq.~\eqref{eq:gj-proportional},
the mixed amplitude is proportional to the tree amplitude. After
including its Wilson coefficient and matching the normalisation,
its interference with the tree amplitude therefore gives a
kinematically constant shift to $V_2$. It changes $v_0$,
but not $v_1$ or $v_2$, and is not included in the results above.


\subsection{Use in higher order calculations}
\label{sec:use}

For the two-body final state, $m_{AA}=\sqrt{\hat s}=2\mA/\sqrt{1-\bA^2}$.
The range $\bA\leq\RecommendedBeta$ therefore covers pair invariant masses
up to $\ThresholdPercentPointTwo\%$ above threshold; for
$\mA=\BenchMA\,\mathrm{GeV}$, this is $m_{AA}\leq\SqrtSPointTwo\,\mathrm{GeV}$.
The point $\bA=\EdgeBeta$ lies $\ThresholdPercentPointThree\%$ above threshold.
%
%Since $m_{AA}=\sqrt{\hat s}=2\mA/\sqrt{1-\bA^2}$, the range $\bA\leq\RecommendedBeta$ covers pair invariant masses up to $\ThresholdPercentPointTwo\%$ above threshold, that is $m_{AA}\leq\SqrtSPointTwo\,\mathrm{GeV}$ for $\mA=\BenchMA\,\mathrm{GeV}$, and $\bA=\EdgeBeta$ lies $\ThresholdPercentPointThree\%$ above threshold.
I
recommend the series through $\bA^6$ for $0<\bA\leq\RecommendedBeta$ at the
angles tested in Tables~\ref{tab:validation-grid} and
\ref{tab:forward-angles}; at $\bA=\EdgeBeta$, the largest relative error of
the complete coefficients on the validation grid is \PtwoErrAtPointThree{}.
These checks do not establish a uniform accuracy bound as $c\to\pm1$, where
the logarithms diverge. The series retain the complex values of both tensor
coefficients, so they can be used in interference with other amplitudes,
provided these are combined with the same normalisation, Wilson coefficient
conventions, tensor basis and infrared subtraction scheme.

One application is the hard function of a threshold resummed or
soft-virtual prediction for the pair invariant mass distribution. There,
threshold refers to the limit of little energy available for additional
radiation at fixed $m_{AA}$, not to
$\bA\to0$~\cite{Sterman:1986aj,Catani:1989ne,deFlorian:2015moa}. The soft
and collinear factors are universal for gluon fusion into a colour singlet,
and the process dependence enters through the finite virtual contributions
at each $m_{AA}$~\cite{deFlorian:2012za,Bhattacharya:2021nsv}; the series
supply the two-loop Type~II input near $m_{AA}=2\mA$. When the Type~II tree
amplitude is the leading amplitude, the required combinations are those of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:virtual}, and integrated over the angle at fixed $m_{AA}$
they give the averages of Eq.~\eqref{eq:angle-average};
Ref.~\cite{deFlorian:2015moa} gives the corresponding construction for
Higgs pairs.

The order at which these remainders enter depends on the leading amplitude
(Sec.~\ref{sec:counting}). For a pseudoscalar coupled to gluons only
through $G\tilde G$, they enter the NNLO virtual contribution. In a 2HDM, they first contribute at N$^3$LO, through interference with the leading
Type~I amplitude. Eq.~\eqref{eq:angle-average} then cannot
be used directly, since the interference with Type~I must be formed before
the angular integration, and the remaining amplitudes, including any mixed
$O_G$--$O_J$ contribution, must be included at the same order. For Higgs
pairs, similarly, the diagrams with two effective vertices enter the NNLO
virtual corrections only at tree level and at one
loop~\cite{deFlorianMazzitelli2013Virtual}, and their two-loop terms are
an ingredient of N$^3$LO~\cite{Banerjee:2018lfq}.
