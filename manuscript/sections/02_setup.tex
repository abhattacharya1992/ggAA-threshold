\section{Amplitudes and conventions}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Effective theory and diagram classes}

We use the conventions of Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd}. After the top quark is integrated out, the pseudoscalar couples to gluons and light quarks through
\begin{align}
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}^A
 &=\Phi_A\left[-\frac{1}{8}C_G O_G-\frac{1}{2}C_J O_J\right],
 \label{eq:effective-lagrangian}\\
 O_G&=\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}G^{a\mu\nu}G^{a\rho\sigma},
 &
 O_J&=\partial_\mu\!\left(\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\gamma_5\psi\right),
 \label{eq:effective-operators}
\end{align}
with $C_G=-2^{5/4}\as G_F^{1/2}\cot\beta_H$, $\as=\alpha_s/(4\pi)$ and $C_J=\Ord(\as)\,C_G$~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd}. Operator mixing, ultraviolet renormalisation and the treatment of $\gamma_5$~\cite{Larin1993} follow the original calculation; the same effective theory underlies inclusive pseudoscalar production at NNLO~\cite{HarlanderKilgore2002}.

Type~I consists of the diagrams with a single effective vertex that produces both pseudoscalars, through a contact term or an intermediate scalar; these vertices are not generated by Eq.~\eqref{eq:effective-lagrangian} alone. Type~II consists of the diagrams in which each pseudoscalar is emitted from its own vertex, with the vertices connected by gluon exchange in the $t$ or $u$ channel (Fig.~\ref{fig:diagrams}). The labels Type~I and Type~II name diagram classes, as in Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd}, and are unrelated to the Yukawa types of two-Higgs-doublet models; in the full theory the Type~II diagrams are the one-particle reducible diagrams of Ref.~\cite{Baglio:2023euv}, two top quark triangles connected by a gluon. We consider the Type~II diagrams with two $O_G$ vertices and drop the superscript $II$ from now on. The Type~II diagrams with one $O_G$ and one $O_J$ vertex start at one loop, where the quark loop attached to $O_J$ couples to the gluons. The published one-loop results of Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd} for these diagrams are exactly proportional to the tree amplitude of Eq.~\eqref{eq:born} below,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal M^{(1),\Fin}_{i,GJ}=\GJRatio\,\nf\,\mathcal M_i^{(0)},\qquad i=1,2,
 \label{eq:gj-proportional}
\end{equation}
at all phase space points. This is what one expects from the axial anomaly~\cite{Adler:1969gk,Bell:1969ts}: for massless quarks the divergence of the axial current equals, at one loop, a multiple of $G\tilde G$, so that the one-loop matrix elements of $O_J$ are proportional to the tree-level matrix elements of $O_G$. The renormalised form of this relation in dimensional regularisation, with the treatment of $\gamma_5$ used here, is given in Ref.~\cite{Larin1993}. These diagrams therefore have no structure of their own near threshold.

\subsection{Tensor decomposition and helicity amplitudes}

The amplitude is decomposed as
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}_{ab}
 =\delta_{ab}\left(T_1^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{M}_1+T_2^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{M}_2\right),
 \label{eq:tensor-decomposition}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{align}
 T_1^{\mu\nu}
 &=g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{p_1^\nu p_2^\mu}{p_1\!\cdot p_2},
 \label{eq:tensor-one}\\
 T_2^{\mu\nu}
 &=g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{(p_1\!\cdot p_2)p_T^2}
 \Bigl[
 \mA^2p_2^\mu p_1^\nu
 -2(p_1\!\cdot p_3)p_2^\mu p_3^\nu
 \nonumber\\[-2pt]
 &\hspace{3.2em}
 -2(p_2\!\cdot p_3)p_3^\mu p_1^\nu
 +2(p_1\!\cdot p_2)p_3^\mu p_3^\nu
 \Bigr],
 \label{eq:tensor-two}\\
 p_T^2&=\frac{tu-\mA^4}{\hat s}.
 \label{eq:pt-definition}
\end{align}
For physical gluon polarisations $T_1$ contributes only to equal helicities ($J_z=0$) and $T_2$ only to opposite helicities ($|J_z|=2$), each with a contraction of unit modulus. Up to phases the helicity amplitudes are
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal A_{\pm\pm}=C_G^2\,\mathcal M_1,
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_{\pm\mp}=C_G^2\,\mathcal M_2\,e^{\pm2i\phi},
 \label{eq:helicity}
\end{equation}
where $\phi$ is the azimuth of $\vec p_3$, and $\sum_{\mathrm{hel,col}}|\mathcal A|^2=2(\Nc^2-1)\,C_G^4\,(|\mathcal M_1|^2+|\mathcal M_2|^2)$. The labels 1 and 2 refer to the tensors, not to the diagram classes.

At tree level the two Type~II diagrams give
\begin{align}
 \mathcal M_1^{(0)}&=\frac{i\hat s}{2},
 \nonumber\\
 \mathcal M_2^{(0)}&=\frac{i\mA^2(x-z)(x^2+1)(xz-1)}{4xz(x^2-xz+1)}
 \nonumber\\
 &=2i\mA^2\bA^2(1-c^2)+\Ord(\bA^4),
 \label{eq:born}
\end{align}
in the variables of Eq.~\eqref{eq:xyz-definition}. We obtained Eq.~\eqref{eq:born} from the projections of the original calculation and checked it against a direct evaluation of the two Feynman diagrams with the vertex $iC_G\varepsilon_{\mu\alpha\rho\beta}k_1^\mu k_2^\rho\delta^{ab}$ that follows from Eq.~\eqref{eq:effective-lagrangian} (App.~\ref{app:normalization}). The suppression of $\mathcal M_2^{(0)}$ by $\bA^2$ is the centrifugal barrier of the $|J_z|=2$ state.

At two loops we separate the powers of the number of light flavours,
\begin{equation}
 \Mfull{i}=\Mflav{i}{0}+\nf\,\Mflav{i}{1}+\nf^2\,\Mflav{i}{2}.
 \label{eq:nf-decomposition}
\end{equation}
These are algebraic coefficients, not separate subprocesses. The complete coefficient below means the sum for $\nf=\BenchNf$.

\subsection{Kinematics}

The Mandelstam variables are
\begin{align}
 \hat s&=(p_1+p_2)^2,
 &t&=(p_1-p_3)^2,
 &u&=(p_2-p_3)^2,
 \label{eq:mandelstam}
\end{align}
with $\hat s+t+u=2\mA^2$. The expressions of Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd} use
\begin{align}
 \hat s&=\mA^2\frac{(1+x)^2}{x},
 &t&=-\mA^2 y,
 &u&=-\mA^2 z,
 \label{eq:xyz-definition}
\end{align}
with $x+x^{-1}=y+z$. With $c=\cos\theta$ in the partonic centre of mass frame,
\begin{align}
 \hat s&=\frac{4\mA^2}{1-\bA^2},
 &t,u&=\mA^2-\frac{\hat s}{2}(1\mp\bA c),
 \label{eq:stu-beta}
\end{align}
and the threshold substitution is
\begin{align}
 x&=\frac{1-\bA}{1+\bA},
 &y,z&=\frac{1+\bA^2\mp2\bA c}{1-\bA^2}.
 \label{eq:threshold-map}
\end{align}
All three variables tend to one as $\bA\to0^+$ at fixed $c$, and the expansion has to follow this correlated limit. The Gram determinant and the transverse momentum are
\begin{equation}
 tu-\mA^4
 =\frac{4\mA^4\bA^2(1-c^2)}{(1-\bA^2)^2},
 \qquad
 p_T^2=\frac{\mA^2\bA^2(1-c^2)}{1-\bA^2}.
 \label{eq:gram-scaling}
\end{equation}
Both vanish at threshold at every angle, and also at fixed $\bA$ when $c\to\pm1$.

\subsection{Finite remainders and normalisation}
\label{sec:normalization}

The coefficients $\mathcal M_i$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:helicity}, from which $C_G^2$ has been factored out, are expanded as
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{M}_i
 =\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_i
 +\as\mathcal{M}^{(1)}_i
 +\as^2\mathcal{M}^{(2)}_i+\cdots,
 \label{eq:perturbative-expansion}
\end{equation}
with $\as$ renormalised at $\muR$. The finite remainders are defined by the infrared subtraction of Refs.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd,Catani:1998bh},
\begin{align}
 \mathcal M^{(1)}&=2I^{(1)}\mathcal M^{(0)}+\mathcal M^{(1),\Fin},
 \nonumber\\
 \mathcal M^{(2)}&=4I^{(2)}\mathcal M^{(0)}+2I^{(1)}\mathcal M^{(1)}+\mathcal M^{(2),\Fin}.
 \label{eq:catani}
\end{align}
The remainders released with Ref.~\cite{Bhattacharya:2019bmd} are not all in the normalisation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:perturbative-expansion}. First, the two-loop remainders are larger by a factor of $\FileFactor$: in the two-loop renormalisation step of the original calculation the $O_G$--$O_G$ amplitude, including the tree and one-loop terms that enter the subtraction, was multiplied by $\FileFactor=\FileFactorRoot^2$. Second, the one-loop remainders do not depend on $\muR$; they contain $\ln\mA^2$ where $\ln(\mA^2/\muR^2)$ is required. The remainders in the normalisation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:perturbative-expansion} are therefore
\begin{align}
 \mathcal M_i^{(1),\Fin}&=\mathcal M_{i,\mathrm{publ}}^{(1),\Fin}+2\beta_0\ln\frac{\muR^2}{\mathrm{GeV}^2}\,\mathcal M_i^{(0)},
 \nonumber\\
 \Mfull{i}&=\frac{1}{\FileFactor}\,\mathcal M_{i,\mathrm{publ}}^{(2),\Fin},
 \label{eq:normalisation}
\end{align}
with $\beta_0=(11\Nc-2\nf)/3$ and masses in GeV in the published expressions. The one-loop term restores the scale dependence required by renormalisation group invariance; with it, the released one- and two-loop remainders are consistent with each other at every tested point. Appendix~\ref{app:normalization} gives the evidence. The logarithmic structure studied below, the expansion and its accuracy do not depend on these corrections; the one-loop constant and the finite virtual corrections of Sec.~\ref{sec:rate} do.

\subsection{Counting of orders and range of validity}
\label{sec:counting}

Since $C_G\propto\as$, the Type~II amplitude starts at $\Ord(\as^2)$, one order higher than an amplitude with a single effective vertex. In a two-Higgs-doublet model the leading order amplitude for $gg\to AA$ is of the Type~I kind, and the Type~II tree amplitude first contributes through its interference with it at NLO. In the NLO calculations of Refs.~\cite{DawsonDittmaierSpira1998,Baglio:2023euv} this is the one-particle reducible contribution to the virtual corrections. The two-loop Type~II remainders then enter at N$^3$LO, like the corresponding class of diagrams for Higgs pairs~\cite{Banerjee:2018lfq}. For a pseudoscalar that couples to gluons only through $G\tilde G$, such as an axion-like particle without further interactions, the Type~II diagrams are the complete tree amplitude and the present results are the $O_G$--$O_G$ part of the two-loop virtual corrections at NNLO; in that case no heavy particle is integrated out in the amplitudes, and the results do not rely on the heavy top quark limit. Since $O_G$ mixes with $O_J$ under renormalisation, an $O_J$ contribution enters at the same order; by Eq.~\eqref{eq:gj-proportional} it is proportional to the tree amplitude. If dimension-six contact interactions are present, as in the analysis of Ref.~\cite{Brivio:2026alp}, they play the role of Type~I.

The effective theory requires the pseudoscalar and the pair invariant mass to be small compared with the top quark threshold, which near the pair threshold means $\mA\lesssim m_t$. For the Type~II tree amplitude the full top quark mass dependence is known: each top quark triangle couples one pseudoscalar to an on-shell and a virtual gluon, and the amplitude of each channel is multiplied by $[I_2(\tau_A,\lambda)/I_2^{\infty}]^2$, with $\tau_A=4m_t^2/\mA^2$, $\lambda=4m_t^2/t$ or $4m_t^2/u$, and the loop function $I_2$ of $A\to Z\gamma$ given in Ref.~\cite{Baglio:2023euv}. At threshold, $t=u=-\mA^2$, this factor is \TopMassRatioA, \TopMassRatioB{} and \TopMassRatioC{} for $\mA=\TopMassMAA$, \TopMassMAB{} and \TopMassMAC~GeV, with $m_t=\BenchMt$~GeV. Near threshold the heavy top quark limit of the Type~II tree amplitude is therefore accurate to about \TopMassPercentMax\% or better for $\mA\lesssim m_t$. For the loop corrections a partial estimate follows from the factorisation discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:dps}: the effective vertices enter the DPS logarithm only through the amplitudes for $gg\to A$ with both gluons on shell, the limit $\lambda\to\infty$ of $I_2$, where $I_2/I_2^\infty=\tau_Af(\tau_A)$ with $f(\tau)=\arcsin^2(1/\sqrt\tau)$. If this factorisation carries over to the full theory, in which the top quark triangles are hard subgraphs, the coefficient of the logarithm is multiplied by $[\tau_Af(\tau_A)]^2=$ \TopMassDPSA, \TopMassDPSB{} and \TopMassDPSC{} for the same masses, while its dependence on the velocity and the helicity selection rule are unchanged. We have no estimate for the remaining terms. For $\mA\gtrsim m_t$ these factors grow quickly, and the heavy top quark limit should not be used there.
